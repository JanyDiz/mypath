# Research

Competitive landscape and rubric-framework research for **mypath** — an AI interview-prep app focused on design (UX/UI/product) and product-management roles. Verified June 2026 via multi-source web research with adversarial fact-checking.

## TL;DR

The niche is **validated but not empty**. Two genuine direct competitors exist (Mockin, UXMock.io), plus general AI tools (Yoodli, Exponent) and free human coaching (ADPList). Everyone already has *some* rubric — so our bet is **DEPTH of senior, criterion-justified judgment across all three formats in one JD-driven flow**, not "rubrics exist nowhere." Pricing clusters at **$8–25/mo**; human coaching is **$100–300/session** (the value anchor).

## Direct competitors

|------|-------|-------------|---------|

No annual. |
| **UXMock.io** | UX/Product, design-challenge | Paste JD → tailored whiteboard prompt; **real-time spoken** AI interview, timed (15/30/45/60min), dynamic follow-ups; 6-dimension rubric (/10 each + overall). | Free: 3 sessions/mo; Pro $12.99/mo (15 sessions); credit add-ons. No annual. |

## Indirect / adjacent

- **Exponent** — strong rubric-based AI grading (per-attribute scores + improvement tips from transcript); PM-strong, not design-specialized. The closest analog to our scoring mechanic.
- **Yoodli** — general AI speaking coach; delivery analytics (pacing, filler words), *not* design/PM judgment. $8/mo (Pro) – $20/mo (Advanced); free = 5 lifetime sessions.
- **ADPList** — free human 1:1 coaching incl. design portfolio & PM cases. The "good enough and free" alternative.
- **Paid human coaching** — $100–300/session, packages >$1,000.

## Two assumptions the research KILLED (don't overstate the gap)

Adversarial verification refuted two convenient assumptions:
1. ❌ "Mockin has no design rubric" — false; it runs a portfolio review against design-manager expectations.
2. ❌ "Exponent has no design support" — false; couldn't confirm absence.

**Implication:** differentiation must rest on **depth of judgment + breadth of formats in one JD-driven flow**, not on competitors lacking rubrics entirely. UXMock's rubric is *generic* (6 dims); Mockin leans STAR/behavioral; Exponent isn't design-specialized. No one combines portfolio walkthrough + behavioral + design challenge under one pasted-JD flow with genuinely senior, criterion-justified feedback. **That's the wedge — a quality wedge.**

## Pricing benchmarks

- AI tools cluster **$8–25/mo**; free tiers everywhere (3–5 sessions); **annual is the discount lever** (only Yoodli uses it).
- Human coaching **$100–300/session** is the value anchor.
- **Our positioning:** subscription **~$12–15/mo** + discounted annual + a **job-cycle one-time pass (~$30–45)** — because prep is burst usage (intense for weeks, then abandoned); a pure sub would churn hard.

## Public frameworks to anchor the rubric

- **Design critique / challenge → NN/g Heuristic Evaluation.** Core discipline: every problem justified against a *named principle*, not generic opinion. The structural model for "criterion-justified feedback."
- **PM / design-challenge cases → CIRCLES Method** (Lewis Lin): 7 steps + 6 explicit evaluation criteria (goals/metrics, persona, prioritization, creativity, feasibility, communication) — directly encodable as scoring dimensions.

⚠️ **Caveat:** these are *answer-structuring* frameworks, not *validated interview-scoring rubrics* with inter-rater reliability. Adapting them into a scoring engine is our senior-judgment layer to own — exactly where competitors are thin.

## Open questions

- How deep is UXMock's / Mockin's feedback *in practice*? (Needs hands-on testing, not marketing copy.)
- Best monetization fit: recurring sub vs. job-cycle one-time pass, given burst-usage retention.
- Any validated published scoring rubrics (vs. answer-structuring frameworks) for portfolio walkthrough & PM behavioral?
